## Description

The squared horizontal and vertical coordinates of a unit-circle point add to one. This identity lets either sine or cosine determine the magnitude of the other.

## Conditions

Any real angle θ.

## Statement

$$
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta = 1.
$$

## History

This is the circle-coordinate form of the Pythagorean theorem, connecting triangle geometry with trigonometric functions.

## Worked example

At θ = π/6: (1/2)² + (√3/2)² = 1.

## Proof sketch

The unit-circle point has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ) and distance 1 from the origin. Apply the Pythagorean theorem.
