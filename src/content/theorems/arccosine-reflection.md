## Description

Negating a cosine input reflects its principal angle across a right angle. The corresponding unit-circle points have opposite horizontal coordinates.

## Conditions

−1 ≤ x ≤ 1.

## Statement

$$
\arccos(-x) = \pi-\arccos x.
$$

## History

The complementary-angle origins of cosine connect its modern inverse identities with circle geometry.

## Worked example

arccos(−1/2) = 2π/3.

## Proof sketch

If θ = arccos x, then cos(π − θ) = −x. Since π − θ remains in [0, π], it is the principal angle for −x.
